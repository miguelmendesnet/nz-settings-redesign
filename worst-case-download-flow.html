Every gate, in sequence.

This is the most loaded scenario: a guest is already inside a password-protected gallery, clicks Download all, and the system has to walk them through four more gates before the zip starts building. Gallery-wide gates are resolved first — so a guest who fails them never has to surrender image-level credentials — then per-image gates follow.

Why this is the painful one

The guest hits four separate gate dialogs after clicking a single button. Each is independently meaningful — they protect different things — but stacked back-to-back they read as friction.

Ordering principles applied:

  • Gallery-wide gates resolve first. A guest who can't pass the gallery PIN never has to surrender image-level credentials.
  • Identity before per-asset secrets. Within the per-image phase, login runs first — it's a cheap question ("is this even your gallery?") and skipping it auto-skips the role-protected photos so we never ask for credentials we won't need.

Still worth questioning

  • Consolidation: can the image-password and image-PIN prompts (steps 4 and 5) be merged into a single "unlock protected photos" step that shows both fields at once? The role/login stays separate because it's an identity gate, not a credential-per-asset gate.
  • Skip-ahead default: for guests who only want the public photos, "Skip" should arguably be the prominent action on per-image prompts, not the secondary one.
  • Progress indicator: a "1 of 4 unlock steps" counter at the top of each modal would make the length of the flow legible up front so guests can decide whether to commit.
